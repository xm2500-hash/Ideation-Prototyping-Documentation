Ideation · Prototyping · Documentation

Xiaoyan Ma / Week 2

50 Ways of Seeing

Subject of Observation

This is the original object that I chose to observe and reinterpret in 50 different ways.

01 — Connected Structure

I used simple blocks in Figma to show the object’s basic structure, including its irregular shapes and how its individual parts are connected.

02 — Pixelated Form

I created a pixel drawing in Photoshop using many small squares. I think the appearance of pixel art matches the overall form of the installation.

03 — Digital Frame Installation

I created multiple photo frames in Blender and arranged them together to reinterpret the structure of the original installation in three-dimensional space.

04 — Distorted Perspective

I used Blender to recreate the installation from my viewing angle and represent the distorted shape I observed.

05 — Connected Distortion

I used Blender to create a distorted form and connected its parts using the small metal rings found in the original installation.

06 — Endless Structure

The installation gave me a sense of infinity, as if I could never finish looking at it. I extended the model into the distance to create the feeling that it has no visible end.

07 — Greener View

I applied a filter to the image and intensify the green tones that I noticed throughout the installation.

08 — Unusual Stacking

I stacked different photographs together in unusual ways to explore another form of photographic layering.

09 — Mysterious Blur

I wrapped a stone in clay to represent the blurred quality of the photographs. I then used red light to create the mysterious feeling that I experienced from the installation.

10 — Blue Memory

I used the same clay-covered stone but changed the lighting to blue, which is closer to the original appearance and atmosphere of the installation.

11 — Fragmented Light

I wrapped bubble wrap around a light and photographed it. The texture divided the light into many blurred spots, reflecting the repetition and unclear images in the original installation.

12 — Projected View

While lying in bed, I noticed that the view outside was mysteriously projected onto my wall. Its blurred appearance reminded me of the unclear images in the installation.

13 — Ink Smell Map

This experiment is related to smell. I noticed that the installation had a strong ink smell, so I stood at different distances to identify which areas smelled stronger. I found that the smell was stronger near the center. Dark green represents a stronger smell, while lighter green represents a weaker smell.

14 — Paper Frame

I used paper cutting to recreate the square frames of the installation.

15 — Two Continuous Lines

I used two continuous lines to draw the installation in a winding form without lifting the pen.

16 — Rope Reconstruction

I used rope, foam, and postcards to recreate the shape of the installation. I also wrapped the rope into several small loops to represent the metal rings that connect its individual parts.

17 — Labeled Photograph

I recreated the appearance of one of the images in the installation and added text underneath it.

18 — Explosion

I drew an explosion that I observed inside one of the photographs in the installation.

19 — Taped Squares

I used pieces of tape to create a group of squares that reflects the repeated rectangular forms in the installation.

20 — Sewn Frame

I experimented with sewing for the first time and used fabric and thread to create a photo frame.

21 — Human Scale

I made a simple drawing of myself standing in front of the installation to show how large it is compared with my body.

22 — Folded Sculpture

I folded paper into a sculpture to transform the flat qualities of the installation into a three-dimensional form.

23 — Rope Outline

I used rope to construct a simple outline of the installation.

24 — Uncontrolled Paper Clips

This was an unsuccessful experiment. I tried to construct shapes using paper clips, but the paper clips did not behave in the way I expected.

25 — Photograph in Pencil

I used a pencil to draw one of the photographs found in the installation.

26 — Overall Shape

I used a pencil to draw the approximate overall shape of the installation.

27 — Frame Bracelet

I created a bracelet using copper. Its square shape was inspired by the rectangular photo frames, while the circular rings represent the metal rings connecting the frames. The row of rings at the bottom also reflects the structure of the original installation.

28 — Colored Frame

I used colored pencils to draw the rectangular shape of a photo frame.

29 — Soap Fragment

I used small pieces of soap to recreate one of the photographic slides in the installation.

30 — Foam and Bead Frame

I used foam paper and Perler beads to recreate the shape and structure of a photo frame.

31 — Sticky Frame

I used tape and hot glue to recreate a photo frame because the surface of the original installation felt sticky when I touched it.

32 — Watercolor Palette

I used watercolor to recreate the main colors of the installation: green, blue, and the white spaces between the images.

33 — Rectangular Perspective

I photographed my watercolor paints from a specific angle because their rectangular shapes and this perspective reminded me of how I viewed the installation.

34 — Watercolor Slide

I used watercolor to recreate one of the photographic slides in the installation.

35 — Blurred Frame

I covered the same photo frame with several layers of plastic wrap to create a blurred appearance.

36 — Failed Golden Age

This was an unsuccessful experiment. I originally wanted to use glue and gold foil to represent the golden age shown in the installation, but the glue was difficult to shape and did not produce the result I expected.

37 — Infinite Hot Glue Frame

I used a hot glue gun to create an infinite frame, translating the endless structure from my earlier digital model into a physical material.

38 — Mirrored Cat Portrait

I wanted to experiment with a mirror, so I used it to reflect my cat and treated the mirror as a photo frame.

39 — Cat Treat Composition

I used the different colors and positions of cat treats on a board to recreate the arrangement of the installation.

40 — Dusty Grid

I created a grid with masking tape and placed dark particles inside some of the squares. The repeated squares represent the photo frames, while the uneven particles reflect the aged and dusty surface of the installation.

41 — Cigarette Frame

I arranged cigarettes into the shape of a photo frame to reinterpret the rectangular structure of the installation.

42 — Disliked Smell

I placed ink on paper because I disliked the strong smell of ink in the installation. I also drew a cat to express my negative reaction to the smell.

43 — Unpleasant Odor

I used a marker to represent the unpleasant smell of the installation. The brown curved lines show the odor spreading through the air.

44 — Sticky Surface

I used my hand and glue to express the sticky feeling I experienced when touching the installation.

45 — Perler Bead Frames

I used Perler beads in similar colors to recreate the repeated photo frames in the installation.

46 — Adhesive Touch

I attached tape to my hand to represent the sticky feeling of touching the installation.

47 — Before the Exhibition

I imagined how the work was stored before it was exhibited, so I asked the person who created it. I learned that its parts were simply stacked together, so I arranged several objects to recreate this stacked state.

48 — Unconnected Rings

I recreated the appearance of the metal rings before they were used to connect the individual parts of the installation.

49 — Connected Beads

I connected the Perler bead frames with metal rings to recreate the connecting system of the original installation.

50 — Fabric Grid

I found several pieces of fabric with grid patterns, combined them, and photographed the resulting arrangement.

At the beginning of this project, only the first ten experiments were completed smoothly. I mainly focused on the visual structure of the installation, such as its rectangular frames, irregular shape, repeated images, and metal rings. My initial experiments used digital tools that I was more familiar with, such as Figma, Photoshop, and Blender, as well as my favorite type of craft project, jewelry making. However, I quickly got stuck, so I gradually stopped focusing only on its appearance and began to think about its smell, sticky surface, light, texture, scale, and the ways its different parts were connected. Around halfway through the process, I began to run out of obvious ideas, so I tried using everyday materials such as rope, clay, bubble wrap, soap, tape, fabric, thread, mirrors, cat treats, and Perler beads. I tried sewing for the first time, as well as many crazy ideas. For example, I was surprised that bubble wrap transformed one light into many blurry spots. The most surprising one was when the view outside was projected onto my wall, which also reminded me of the blurry photographs in the installation.
