## Creative Process

I chose to tell this story through a future child's diary. A child and his companion robot dog, Hulu, discover my time capsule in a park. In this future, people's bodies can be modified, so the child has hard plates installed on his fingertips. I wanted to show the future through these everyday details.

I arranged the narrative across four pages: discovering the package, opening it at home, and examining the four objects. I tried to capture a child's drawing style and voice. After more than a hundred years, many of the objects have become damaged and rusty.  At first, I wrote a lot, but then I thought a child probably would not enjoy writing so much, so I shortened the text. He does not have enough information to understand some of the objects, so he is left with questions.## Reflection

In Part 2, each object had a clear meaning to me, but from the future child's perspective, these meanings could not be fully passed on. The bracelet's scent had disappeared, and the memory of moving that day could not be understood from a scrap of paper saying “Busy” alone. To me, the twine was something my grandfather left behind, but to the child, it was just a tangle of string.

I think my way of preserving things is very personal, so preserving objects does not mean preserving complete memories. The future child did not really know me, so he could only guess, and most of the time, his guesses turned into something different from my original stories.
